The Medium is the Teaching

Why Organizing as Commons Creates Transformation

Like fish discovering water, we can't see our work culture until we step into a different stream. Most of us have spent our entire professional lives swimming in extractive models—isolated practitioners competing for clients, expertise commodified and hoarded, knowledge transmitted top-down from expert to recipient. We don't question it because we can't see it. It's just "how things are."

Present of Work is a different stream. Not an organization, but a commons—a third space where practitioners experimenting with transformation work gather, practice, and learn together. Not to escape the dominant culture, but to make it visible—and therefore changeable.

The Medium is the Message

Marshall McLuhan's most famous insight—"the medium is the message"—means that the form of communication matters more than its content. The printing press didn't just transmit information faster; it fundamentally changed how humans think, relate, and organize society.

Applied to Present of Work: The commons structure (gift-stewarded space, peer learning, distributed authority, collective governance) isn't just a nice container for our teachings. The structure IS the teaching.

When you participate in a gift-stewarded commons, you don't learn about generosity—you embody reciprocity. When you experience peer learning, you don't study horizontal authority—you practice it. When you steward shared resources as commons, you don't conceptualize collective ownership—you live it.

The transformation happens in the organizing, not the ideology.

The Problem with Conventional Professional Development:

An expert lectures about collaboration (hierarchical form, collaborative content) | A consultant sells you tools for distributed decision-making (extractive form, distributed content) | A trainer teaches gift economy principles (transactional form, gift content).

When form and content conflict, form wins. Always.

Creating a Counter-Environment

McLuhan had another crucial insight: You can only see your environment from outside it.

Fish don't know they're in water. They've never experienced anything else. To discover water, you need to jump onto land—to experience a radically different environment that creates contrast.

Most practitioners have never experienced anything other than extractive professional culture. It's so ubiquitous it's invisible:

  • Isolated practice (you're on your own)
  • Commodified expertise (knowledge is sold)
  • Client extraction (get as much revenue as possible)
  • Competition with peers (they're threats, not collaborators)
  • Anxiety-driven work (never enough, must do more)

Present of Work creates what McLuhan called a "counter-environment"—a deliberate alternative that makes the dominant environment visible through contrast.

When you experience gift-stewarded commons, you realize extraction wasn't inevitable. When you participate in peer learning, you see expert-dependence as a choice. When you engage in collective governance, you recognize private ownership as just one option.

The counter-environment doesn't argue against the dominant culture. It reveals it by existing as an alternative.

What Work Can Be: Retrieving Ancient Wisdom

In many cultures and times, work wasn't separate from life. It was how you participated in the world's becoming:

Kammathana (Buddhist Tradition)

Work as occupation of mind—not just physical labor but practice of attention, meditation made manifest. The way you sweep the floor is the way you live your life. Work as spiritual practice.

Ayni (Andean Quechua)

Reciprocal labor—I work in your field, you work in mine. Not transaction (I pay you), but relationship (we care for each other). Built into the structure of work itself.

Amish Barn Raising

Work as community building—when someone needs a barn, the whole community gathers. The work creates the community as much as the barn. Collective capacity building through collective work.

These aren't nostalgic retrievals or historical curiosities. They're patterns that worked for millennia before industrial capitalism made them obsolete. Present of Work as commons retrieves them in contemporary forms appropriate to our moment.

How Structure Shapes Practitioners (And Therefore Organizations)

McLuhan's insight:

"We shape our tools, and thereafter our tools shape us." If we organize as hierarchy, we create hierarchical practitioners. If we organize as market, we create market-minded practitioners. If we organize as commons, we create commoners.

Present of Work is organized as commons because the structure shapes what practitioners embody. When you participate in gift-stewarded space, peer learning, and distributed authority—not as concepts but as lived practice—you carry that in your nervous system. That's what you bring to organizational work.

The Transformation Mechanism:

1. Structure shapes practitioners | When you participate in gift-stewarded commons, peer learning, distributed authority, and collective governance as lived practice, you embody these patterns.

2. Practitioners shape engagements | When you work with organizations or communities, you carry this embodied knowledge. You don't "implement distributed decision-making" as a technique—you hold distributed authority as a way of being.

3. Organizations shape culture | As more organizations experience and embody these patterns, they spread. Not through argument or ideology, but through lived experience.

4. Movement emerges through structure | As more commons emerge, positioned as counter-environments at different edges, the dominant culture becomes increasingly visible. Change accelerates not through converting people but through creating more contrast.

This is why we say: The commons is the teaching.

Movement Strategy: Creating Counter-Environments at Scale

This isn't just about one nice community. It's a movement strategy based on McLuhan's insights:

You can't change a system from inside it. You need counter-environments.

Present of Work as commons is one counter-environment. But the strategy is creating many such spaces—each positioned at different edges, each making different aspects of dominant culture visible.

Think of it as:

  • Acupuncture points in the professional culture
  • Tide pools showing what's possible
  • Probe heads making the dominant environment visible
  • Training grounds for practitioners shaped differently

The goal isn't to "win" against extractive models. It's to make them visible by providing contrast. When enough practitioners have experienced an alternative, the dominant model stops being invisible (and therefore inevitable).

Movement building through form, not ideology.

What This Means for You

If you're reading this, you're probably a practitioner who works with organizations/communities, someone who feels the extractive model doesn't fit, drawn to gift economy but unsure how to sustain yourself, seeking peers rather than experts or competitors.

Here's what to understand:

Present of Work is cool media. It requires your active participation. You won't "take a course" and get the answer. You'll join a commons and help generate what emerges. This is harder than passive consumption. It's also infinitely more transformative.

The commons is the teaching. You don't learn about gift economy—you practice stewarding shared space. You don't study peer learning—you experience it. You don't analyze commons governance—you participate in it. The structure shapes you.

This shapes what you bring to your work. When you've embodied these patterns through lived practice, they become available intuitively in your engagements. Organizations feel the difference.

The transformation spreads through embodiment, not ideology. You're not learning concepts to teach others. You're developing capacity to hold different structures. This is movement building through form.

The Invitation

You can't see the water you're swimming in. But you can step into a different stream.

Present of Work isn't better than conventional professional culture. It's different. And that difference creates the contrast that makes the dominant model visible—and therefore changeable.

We're not arguing against extraction. We're creating a commons that reveals extraction by existing as alternative.

The medium is the message. The commons is the teaching. The form is the transformation.

If this resonates, the question isn't "do I agree?" but "am I willing to step into a different stream and help shape it?"
