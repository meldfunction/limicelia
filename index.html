Can you accompany thresholds
without extracting from them?

Limicelia is part of the Present of Work movement—a network of practitioners who assemble to walk with organizations, communities, and movements through threshold moments.

Limicelia — From Latin limen (threshold) and celia (hidden chamber). The practice of attending to what emerges at boundaries, staying present through becoming without forcing the outcome.

Threshold-crossing isn't change management. It's not implementing solutions or optimizing systems. It's becoming fundamentally different, not just doing things differently. This happens through practice and relationship, not through plans and metrics.

Our Approach

Threshold-crossing is different from problem-solving. Change management optimizes existing systems. Crossing thresholds means questioning the systems themselves—becoming fundamentally different, not just more efficient. This happens through sustained accompaniment, practice, and relationship, not through predetermined plans or quick fixes.

Start With Relationship

How do we begin threshold work?

+

Before strategy, before structure, before new practices—we begin by understanding the relational field. Who trusts whom? Where's the resilience? Where's the friction? Transformation happens through relationship, not around it.

Attend to Emergence

What about planning and strategy?

+

We don't impose predetermined solutions. Instead, we create conditions for what needs to emerge. This requires patience, skill at pattern recognition, and trust that groups hold their own answers—they just need the right containers to discover them.

Build Capacity, Not Dependency

What happens when the work ends?

+

When we leave, you should be more capable than when we arrived. We transfer practices, not just results. We work alongside your team, not above them. The work itself becomes the training ground for new ways of being together.

Integrate Multiple Ways of Knowing

Is intellectual understanding enough?

+

Cognitive understanding is necessary but not sufficient. We engage embodied wisdom, emotional intelligence, relational knowing, and systems awareness. Different situations require different ways of knowing—and groups that can access multiple modes are more resilient.

Work at Multiple Scales

Where does change happen?

+

Personal transformation, team dynamics, organizational culture, network coordination—these aren't separate challenges. They're nested systems. We work across scales simultaneously, recognizing that change at one level affects all others.

Liberation As Practice

What about power and equity?

+

You can't liberate through forms that reproduce domination. Justice and liberation aren't goals we implement—they're qualities that emerge from how we organize. If we use hierarchical methods to create equity, the hierarchy wins. If we extract value to fund regeneration, extraction wins. The form IS the politics.

We practice what Bayo Akomolafe calls "post-activism"—not implementing solutions but creating conditions where other ways of being become possible. Justice isn't a destination we reach by moving faster. As Bayo reminds us: "The times are urgent, let us slow down."

This means justice as right relationship—with each other, with work, with power, with resources. Not just analyzing oppression but practicing liberation through the structures we create. Commons governance. Gift economy. Peer learning. Collective stewardship. These aren't metaphors—they're the actual practices.

The commons we participate in isn't efficient. It's not scalable. It's compost—messy, slow, generative. We're not trying to win against extractive models. We're creating enough different streams that people can discover the water they've been swimming in. Liberation happens through practicing different structures, not through declaring it as goal.

What Accompaniment Produces

Real outcomes from walking with mission-driven organizations through thresholds.

Funder Network Collaboration

The Challenge: A network of funders working on peace and global security struggled to coordinate across different priorities and geographies. They wanted to collaborate but lacked shared frameworks for decision-making and communication.

What We Did: Over 6 months, we facilitated shared decision-making practices, created communication protocols, designed how funders could learn from each other without centralizing decision-making, and built capacity for distributed coordination.

Outcomes: The network renewed their partnership commitment. Decision quality improved. Participants reported feeling less isolated, more connected to shared mission. They chose to continue the partnership and expand their collaboration.

What "Accompaniment" Means

We don't parachute in with recommendations. We work alongside your team—listening more than telling, building your capacity rather than creating dependency, treating the work itself as the practice where becoming happens.

After we leave, you have not just new practices, but the relational foundation and distributed understanding to evolve them. The shifts persist because your people own them.

About Present of Work

Present of Work is a learning commons—a third space where practitioners exploring work as participation in life on earth gather to practice gift economy, peer learning, and collective stewardship. Like fish discovering water, we can't see our work culture until we step into a different stream.

The name holds three meanings: work as present (gift), being present (attentive), making alternatives present (manifest by embodying them).

This isn't random organizing—it's creating a counter-environment to extractive professional culture. The medium is the message. The commons doesn't teach these principles—it IS them. Structure shapes practitioners. Practitioners shape organizations. This is how becoming spreads.
