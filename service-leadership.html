Section 01

Hero Headline

[ When the ________ between what is said and what is real becomes ________. ]

what goes here

What belongs here

One sentence in the client's frame — the situation they are in, not the service you offer. For leadership work, this is almost always about a gap: between espoused values and actual behavior, between what leadership says and what culture does.

Not "We help leaders become more effective." Instead: the sentence that makes the right client feel seen.

Section 02

Lede — who it's for

[ Two sentences. Who. What shifts. ]

what goes here

What belongs here

Two sentences maximum. First: who specifically — what kind of leader, organization, or moment. Second: what changes as a result. The test: does this describe a real transformation or a consulting aspiration?

Leadership work is personal. The lede needs to hold that — not hide behind "teams and leaders."

Section 03

Entry Scenarios

[ 3–4 cards. Real situations. Not diagnoses. ]

what goes here

What belongs here

Situations a client is in when they reach out — written as they would describe them, not as a practitioner would diagnose them. Leadership and culture entries are often emotional, relational, exhausted. Let the scenarios hold that.

Best source: what clients say in the first 10 minutes of a first call. Not what they say they need — what they describe happening.

Section 04

Engagement Types

[ 3–4 types. Named by what they produce. ]

what goes here

What belongs here

The actual work — named by what it produces, not the methodology behind it. For leadership work, this includes both the inner and the outer: what changes in the leader, what changes in the system around them.

Do not say "executive coaching." Say what actually happens in it and what comes out the other side.

Section 05

Contrast

[ What the market offers vs. what we do. ]

what goes here

What belongs here

An honest comparison. The leadership development market is enormous and full of approaches that don't stick. If Limicelia's approach is genuinely different, this section earns its place. If not, cut it.

The somatic dimension is real and rare. The inner-outer integration is unusual. Name those specifically — don't just say "we go deeper."

Section 06

Related Domains

[ 2–3 adjacent domains that travel with this. ]

what goes here

What belongs here

Adjacent domains that often arise from or alongside leadership work. Only list genuine relationships — not a tour of all Limicelia services. Leadership and Org Design almost always travel together. Conflict often surfaces through leadership gaps.

The test: would a client in the middle of this work naturally encounter the adjacent domain? If yes, list it.

Section 07

Invitation

[ Not a pitch. An honest next step. ]

what goes here

What belongs here

A short paragraph. The emotional register after reading a leadership page is often one of recognition and relief — someone finally named what's been happening. The invitation should meet that state. Not "book a call." Something more like: "tell us what's not working."

Email only. No Calendly on prod. The invitation should feel human, not transactional.

B / Live Example — Leadership & Culture Transformation

What it looks like
filled in.

Current working draft. Read for what rings true and what doesn't. This is the content that needs to be tested against real client language before publishing.

Live draft · Leadership & Culture · review and revise

01 · Hero Headline

"When the gap between what leadership says and what culture does has become the organization's primary problem."

02 · Lede

For executives and teams doing the outer work of change while avoiding the inner work that makes it stick. We work with leaders and systems simultaneously — because culture doesn't change from the top down or the bottom up; it changes at the place where the two meet.

03 · Entry Scenarios

The executive who says all the right things in the all-hands and then does the opposite in 1:1s.
The team exhausted not from the work but from the gap between what's said and what's real.
A leadership transition that revealed how much of the culture lived in one person's way of being.

04 · Engagement Types

Executive Accompaniment — Working one-on-one with leaders through significant change. Inner and outer work together. The practitioner's presence is an instrument of this work.

Culture Repair — For organizations where the relational fabric has frayed through rapid growth, leadership failure, or accumulated harm. We build what's needed now.

Team Recalibration — When a team has lost alignment not on strategy but on how they are together. Facilitated process for naming what's true and designing forward.

05 · Contrast

What most leadership development does: Workshop-based skill building. Personality frameworks. 360 feedback with no follow-through.

What we do: Work at the level of embodied presence — not just thinking. What a leader can hold in their body determines what the organization can hold in its culture. We work with the thing underneath behaviors.

06 · Related Domains

Organizational Design & Governance — Culture changes rarely hold without parallel structural work. These almost always travel together.

Conflict Resolution — Leadership gaps frequently surface as relational rupture. Sometimes conflict is the entry point into the deeper leadership work.

07 · Invitation

If something here named what you've been trying to describe — that's usually the sign. Tell us what's happening. We'll tell you honestly if this is the work.

C / Co-Steward Prompts

Questions to answer
together.

Your answers become the content. Work in order. Some questions will surface disagreement — that's the point.

Section 01 · Hero Headline

What does a client actually say when they first describe why they're calling about leadership or culture? What are the exact words?

Not "we have a culture problem." What do they actually say? That language is the headline.

Section 01 · Hero Headline

Is the entry to this work most often a leadership problem, a culture problem, or a relational problem — and how do clients distinguish between those, if at all?

The headline should reflect the real entry. It's often not what the practitioner would call it.

Section 02 · Lede

The current lede mentions "inner and outer work simultaneously." Is that language that clients recognize, or is it practitioner language that would confuse them?

If it would confuse a potential client, it needs to be replaced with what they already understand.

Section 03 · Entry Scenarios

What are the two or three real situations you've been called into for leadership and culture work? Describe them as the client would — not as you would diagnose them.

The draft scenarios are written in practitioner voice. Replace with real situations if you have them.

Section 04 · Engagement Types

Are the three engagement types (Executive Accompaniment, Culture Repair, Team Recalibration) the right names and the right scope? What's missing?

Name only what you're currently offering and genuinely able to deliver. Not aspirational scope.

Section 04 · Engagement Types

The somatic dimension is named in the contrast section but not in the engagement descriptions. Should it appear in the engagements themselves? How explicitly?

Some clients will be drawn to it. Others will be confused or put off. Where does it belong on the page?

Section 05 · Contrast

The current contrast says "We work at the level of the practitioner's embodied presence." Is that language right for this audience? Too insider?

The contrast section only works if it names the distinction in a way a skeptical client would recognize, not a way that reads like a methodology description.

Overall

Is there anything on this page that overstates what's currently being offered, or that you'd feel uncomfortable being held to by a prospective client?

That's the editorial floor. If yes, cut it before the page goes live.
