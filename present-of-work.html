Present of Work

A learning commons for practitioners exploring work as participation in life on earth—not extraction from it. Third space where we practice gift economy, peer learning, and collective stewardship as lived experience.

Threshold-crossing isn't change management. It's becoming fundamentally different, not just doing things differently. The commons itself is the threshold, not a tool for crossing it elsewhere.

Three Questions About Work

What is work when it's alive?

Not occupational labor that some enjoy or endure, but work that makes you MORE yourself, more connected, more capable. Work where you learn and grow. Work that serves something larger while nourishing who you are.

Ancient traditions called this different things: Kammathana (Buddhist - work as occupation of mind, spiritual practice), Ayni (Andean - work as reciprocal community weaving), Gelassenheit (Amish - work as yielding to collective need).

What is work when it's submerging?

The traditions that saw work as extension of life on earth. Work as craft and calling. The understanding that how we work shapes who we become.

We've fragmented the question: Reimagining the world (SDGs) without reimagining work. Reinventing organizations (new structures) without reimagining ourselves. Transforming systems without transforming how we show up.

What holds us back from living work?

The belief that work is separate from life. That inner development happens over here, organizational change over there, systems transformation somewhere else entirely. That we can change the world without changing how we work.

Present of Work begins here: Work is the bridge. Not separate from becoming but the site of it. How we work shapes who we become. And who we become shapes what's possible.

A Counter-Environment to Extractive Professional Culture

Present of Work is a learning commons—a counter-environment where practitioners experimenting with threshold work gather, practice, and learn together. Like fish discovering water, we can't see our work culture until we step into a different stream. This is that different stream.

Think of it as a third space—not work, not home, but where practitioners gather to experiment. Like a park where some practice tai chi, others hold dialogue circles, still others test new organizing models. Not owned by any one organization; collectively stewarded as commons.

The name holds three meanings: work as present (gift), being present (attentive), making alternatives present (manifest by embodying them).

The medium is the message. The commons doesn't teach gift economy—it is gift-stewarded. It doesn't explain peer learning—practitioners practice as peers. It doesn't advocate for commons governance—it operates as commons. The structure itself teaches by existing as a living alternative.

This isn't random organizing—it's deliberate positioning at the edge where different systems meet. Nonprofits ↔ Social enterprises. Practitioners ↔ Organizations. Sacred ↔ Economic. Individual ↔ Collective. Edges are the most fertile zones. The commons sits at that boundary, creating contrast that makes dominant patterns visible and therefore changeable.

Justice Through Form, Not Ideology

You can't liberate through forms that reproduce domination. If we organize as hierarchy to create equity, the hierarchy wins. If we extract value to fund regeneration, extraction wins. The form IS the politics.

This is what Bayo Akomolafe calls "post-activism"—not implementing justice as a goal but creating conditions where liberation becomes possible through practice. Justice as right relationship with work, with each other, with power, with resources. The commons doesn't declare liberation—it practices it through gift-stewarding, peer learning, collective governance.

"The times are urgent, let us slow down." The commons is compost—messy, slow, generative. Not efficient. Not scalable. Not trying to win against extractive models but creating cracks where alternatives can grow.

What You Might Find in the Commons

Different experiences emerge in different seasons, stewarded by various practitioners. The commons itself is collectively maintained.

🌱 Always Accessible

The base layer: Monthly practice spaces (CommDojo, Conflict Kinship Dojo, Soul Space), open conversations, peer learning circles, shared tools. Like public paths in a park—anyone can walk them. Gift-stewarded, freely accessible.

🪢 Seasonal Intensives

Concentrated practice: When practitioners organize learning cohorts—8-12 week deep dives in specific territories. Retrieving the guild tradition for threshold work. Emerge based on need and collective energy.

🕸️ Edge Encounters

Movement connections: Transformation labs where the commons meets wider movements. Cross-pollination with other communities experimenting with new organizing models.

How Structure Shapes Practitioners (And Therefore Organizations)

McLuhan's insight: "We shape our tools, and thereafter our tools shape us." If we organize as hierarchy, we create hierarchical practitioners. If we organize as market, we create market-minded practitioners. If we organize as commons, we create commoners.

Present of Work is organized as commons because the structure shapes what practitioners embody. When you participate in gift-stewarded space, peer learning, and distributed authority—not as concepts but as lived practice—you carry that in your nervous system. That's what you bring to organizational work.

How Limicelia Participates

Limicelia is one practitioner stewarding this commons, among others. We help maintain infrastructure, participate in learning, bring what we discover to consulting work. It's a living cycle:

Organizations with capacity hire Limicelia for threshold work → Consulting revenue helps steward commons infrastructure → Practitioners gather, learn, experiment in the commons → Embodied experience shapes how we work with organizations → Becoming spreads through practice, not ideology.

When Limicelia practitioners work with your organization, we bring lived experience from this commons. We've practiced distributed authority with peers, participated in gift-stewarded spaces, engaged in collective governance. Not as theory but as embodied knowing. The commons shapes us; we bring that shaping to the work. This is how becoming spreads—through practitioners shaped by different structures.
