Section 01

Hero Headline

[ When community voice gets laundered into legitimacy — and the actual decisions were already made. ]

what goes here

What belongs here

One sentence in the client's frame. But for this domain, the client is often the problem — they may not know they're doing consultation theater. The headline has to name the failure mode in a way that an honest client recognizes themselves in, without alienating them.

The paradox: the clients who most need this work are least likely to call it "community engagement problems." They call it "we need help with our stakeholder process." What do they actually say?

Section 02

Lede — who it's for

[ Two sentences. Who. What shifts. ]

what goes here

What belongs here

Who specifically — what kind of organization, what kind of design process. And the key distinction: this is not user research. It is a different relationship to who holds authority over the design. The lede should establish that distinction early.

The client who needs this is often a city agency, an NGO doing product/service design, or a government body. What language do they use? Not "co-design" — what do they call it?

Section 03

Entry Scenarios

[ 3–4 real situations. The consultation theater moment. ]

what goes here

What belongs here

The situations that make organizations realize their community engagement isn't working — or that they need a different approach. Often: listening sessions that changed nothing, surveys that produced results nobody used, community advisory boards that exist on paper.

Best entries: specific failures of conventional engagement. The twelve listening sessions. The survey of thousands. Name the pattern — not the organization.

Section 04

Engagement Types

[ 2–3 types. Only what you're currently able to offer. ]

what goes here

What belongs here

For a developing practice, be conservative here. List only what you've done and can do well. The temptation is to describe the full range of participatory design methods — resist it. What specifically is Limicelia offering in this domain right now?

This is one of the most important questions in this guide: what are you actually offering here? Not what the field offers — what can you deliver?

Section 05

Contrast

[ What consultation theater looks like vs. what genuine co-design does. ]

what goes here

What belongs here

The contrast between conventional community engagement (extraction) and genuine co-design (authority shared) is sharp and important. This section may be more necessary here than in any other domain — because so many organizations do the first while calling it the second.

The test: does the contrast section help a prospective client understand what they'd be getting into — and self-select appropriately? If yes, include it.

Section 06

Related Domains

[ 2–3 domains that arise alongside this. ]

what goes here

What belongs here

Community engagement work often surfaces organizational design issues (who holds authority?), field building needs (who else should be in the room?), and leadership questions (is the executive sponsor actually committed?). Name the real relationships.

The test: which adjacent domains would a client in this work naturally run into?

Section 07

Invitation

[ Honest. Specific. Not transactional. ]

what goes here

What belongs here

For a developing practice, the invitation should be more selective. Not "reach out and we'll figure it out" — but something that signals Limicelia takes on this work carefully and chooses engagements where genuine co-design is possible. The invitation can pre-screen.

Something like: "We take on this work selectively — where the conditions for genuine co-design are possible, not just intended."

02 · Lede

For organizations designing services, policies, and products that affect communities who aren't in the room where decisions get made. We work to bring community intelligence into the center of design — not as consultation, but as actual co-design.

03 · Entry Scenarios

A city agency that held twelve listening sessions and changed nothing in the final plan.
A product team that surveyed thousands of users and built what they already planned to build.
Community advisory boards that exist on paper, meet quarterly, and have never influenced a decision.

04 · Engagement Types

[ Needs co-steward input before drafting. ] What specifically is Limicelia offering in this domain right now?

Possible forms: co-design process design, community sensemaking facilitation, frontline intelligence integration. What's real?

05 · Contrast

What consultation theater does: Holds listening sessions. Produces a report. Refers back to the report to justify decisions already made. The community validates, not designs.

What genuine co-design does: Brings community authority into the design process — not just input. Who holds authority over the design? That's the question.

06 · Related Domains

Organizational Design & Governance — Community engagement often surfaces internal authority questions: who inside the org has to agree to act on what the community says?

Field Building — Sometimes the right intervention is bringing more voices into a larger room, not just into one organization's process.

07 · Invitation

We take this on selectively — in contexts where genuine co-design is possible, not just intended. Tell us what you're trying to do. We'll be honest about whether we're the right fit.

For a developing practice, the first question is foundational: are we ready to publish a page at all? Work through this before drafting content.

Before drafting

Are we ready to publish a public page for this domain? What would need to be true — in terms of track record, clarity of offer, or capacity — before we do?

This is the foundational question for all developing practice pages. Publishing creates an expectation. If the expectation can't be met, the page does more harm than good.

Section 04 · Engagement Types

What specifically is Limicelia offering in this domain right now? Name it precisely — not what the field of participatory design does, but what you can and will deliver.

The engagement types section is blank until this is answered. Everything else depends on knowing the actual scope.

Section 01 · Hero Headline

How do clients who have tried conventional engagement processes describe what went wrong? What language do they use?

The headline "consultation theater" is practitioner language. What would a city planner, NGO director, or product team lead actually say?

Section 03 · Entry Scenarios

Have you actually been called into situations like these? If so, describe one — from the client's frame, not as practitioner diagnosis.

If not, the scenarios may be hypothetical — which is a signal that the page isn't ready yet.

Overall

What's the risk of publishing this page before we're ready? What would a mismatched inquiry look like — and can we handle it gracefully?

For developing practices, the invitation section can pre-screen. But only if the selectivity is genuine and consistently applied.
