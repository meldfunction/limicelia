Developing practice — taken on selectively. Convening work is large territory. This guide is about defining the specific edge where Limicelia operates — and what makes it different from event facilitation or conference design.

Domain: Field Building & Ecosystem ConveningStatus: Developing practice

Section 01

Hero Headline

[ When there's something trying to emerge between people who've never been in a room together. ]

what goes here

What belongs here

One sentence in the client's frame. Field building entries often come from a felt sense that something is possible between organizations or practitioners that hasn't been named yet. The headline should name the latent potential, not the convening format.

Not "We design field-building convenings." Instead: what the client feels is possible but hasn't been able to reach yet. What's the thing they sense wants to emerge?

Section 02

Lede — who it's for

[ Two sentences. Who. What shifts. ]

what goes here

What belongs here

Who specifically calls about field building — funders? Ecosystem leaders? Organizations at field edges? And what's the distinction: not networking, not conference, not panel — encounters that change how people think. The lede should hold that distinction in plain language.

The test: does it describe an experience the right client has been wanting to create and couldn't figure out how? Or does it just describe a service?

Section 03

Entry Scenarios

[ 3–4 real situations. The moment of latent potential. ]

what goes here

What belongs here

Situations where an ecosystem or field has enough potential but no container for it yet. Often: people who work in the same space but don't know each other, a field that talks about systems change but convenes in panels, a funder trying to catalyze something they can sense but can't name.

Best entries: the specific latent energy that the convening is designed to release. Not "we needed to bring stakeholders together." What was wanting to happen?

Section 04

Engagement Types

[ What specifically is offered — and at what scale. ]

what goes here

What belongs here

The most important section for this domain. Field building work ranges from a small generative gathering to a multi-year ecosystem strategy. What specifically is Limicelia offering? What's the scale, the format, the commitment? This needs to be precise before the page is published.

Key question: is this single convenings? Multi-year field building strategy? Consultation on convening design? Define the actual offer before drafting this section.

Section 05

Contrast

[ What events and conferences do vs. what we do. ]

what goes here

What belongs here

The distinction between event facilitation (logistics + agenda) and field convening (designing for emergence) is real and important. Name it concretely. What does designing for emergence actually mean? What does harvest facilitation produce? The contrast is only useful if it's specific.

The failure mode: "We don't just do logistics — we design transformative experiences." That's noise. What specifically is different about the design and the outcome?

Section 06

Related Domains

[ 2–3 domains that arise from field work. ]

what goes here

What belongs here

Field building often reveals community engagement needs (who's missing from the room?), governance questions (how does this field make decisions?), and knowledge architecture needs (how does what's learned get held?). Name the real connections.

The test: after a convening, what domains does the work naturally flow into?

Section 07

Invitation

[ Honest. Selective. Specific to the scale of the offer. ]

what goes here

What belongs here

For a developing practice, the invitation should be more selective than for established domains. It can name what Limicelia is looking for in a field building engagement — not just who to call. The right clients will recognize themselves. The wrong ones will self-select out.

Something like: "We're drawn to fields where something is trying to emerge and hasn't found a container yet. Tell us what you're sensing." Not: "We design convenings of all sizes."

What most events produce: Content delivery. Networking time. A feeling of connection that dissipates within a week. The field stays the same.

C / Co-Steward Prompts

Questions to answer
together.

For a developing practice, the foundational question comes first. Everything else depends on it.

Before drafting

What specifically are we offering in this domain? Describe it in one sentence without using the words "design," "facilitation," or "emergence."

This is the most important prompt in this guide. The engagement types section cannot be drafted without answering it. And if you can't answer it in one sentence, the page isn't ready yet.

Section 01 · Hero Headline

What do clients actually say when they describe why they're calling about field building or ecosystem work? What's the first sentence of that conversation?

Often it's not "we need a convening." It's something about a felt gap, a potential not yet realized, a question the field isn't asking together yet. What's the actual entry?

Section 03 · Entry Scenarios

Have you actually been called into a field building or ecosystem convening situation? If so, describe one — from the client's perspective, not as practitioner.

If not, the scenarios are hypothetical — which is important to name before publishing the page.

Section 04 · Engagement Types

Is this work offered as standalone convening design, or does it always connect to broader field-building strategy? What's the smallest engagement you'd take on in this domain?

The answer shapes whether this is a narrow or broad offer — and whether it should be a full one-pager or a paragraph on the services page for now.

Overall

Is this domain ready for a public page — or should it stay as a line on the services page until there's more track record and clarity of offer?

Not a rhetorical question. Some developing practices are better described briefly on the main services page, with "reach out if this resonates," than with their own one-pager that overstates the current offer.
