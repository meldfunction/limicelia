Limicelia · Mission & Impact

Why this work.
What we're building toward.

A brief account of our purpose, what we are trying to change, and how we will know when we are getting somewhere.

01 / Purpose

Human agency
through the threshold.

We help organizations navigate profound technological and structural change in a way that preserves and deepens human agency, trust, and meaning at work. We accompany them through the threshold — not managing them across it, not prescribing the path, but staying present through the hard middle where most transformation work breaks down.

The current dominant model for organizational change — top-down, optimization-focused, often mandated — is producing real harm: eroded trust, talent loss, surface compliance, and decisions made faster than organizations can understand their consequences. This is not inevitable. It is a choice about whose knowledge counts and who holds authority over change.

02 / What we want to change

The positive
we are building toward.

Organizations that absorb change in ways that strengthen — rather than erode — the relationship between leadership and the people doing the work. Where the humans closest to the impact have genuine voice in how technology and structure reshapes their labor, their decisions, and their sense of purpose.

The people we focus on: Senior leaders — CHROs, COOs, heads of transformation — and the employees they are responsible for, particularly those navigating change without adequate support, honest information, or meaningful participation in the decisions being made about their work.

At the organizational level, we work on governance structures, decision-making practices, and the norms that shape how power moves when change is introduced. The people question and the systems question are not separate.

03 / What changes

Five things
we are working toward.

These are the outcomes we are building toward and will hold ourselves accountable to — not abstract aspirations, but the things we will actually look for when an engagement ends.

01

Leaders can name what change is actually doing to their organization — to labor, to decision-making, to power — before committing to a path. Not adoption metrics. The underlying reality.

02

Employees have meaningful participation in designing how change reshapes their work. Architects of redesigned workflows, not subjects of them.

03

Organizations build internal governance capacity — the ongoing ability to make decisions about change well, without permanent dependence on outside support.

04

Trust is preserved or rebuilt through the process of change, not sacrificed to it. The relationship between leadership and workforce comes out stronger.

05

The honest conversation happens. The grief, the uncertainty, the contradictions are named and worked with — rather than managed, suppressed, or spun into a narrative that nobody believes.

04 / Where we are

Building the evidence
as we go.

This practice is developing through live engagements. We are building the evidence base as we work — which is itself a statement about how we think change happens. We don't arrive with a proven model. We arrive with well-grounded methods, genuine curiosity, and a commitment to staying honest about what is and isn't working.

What we are focused on now: deepening the diagnostic practice, launching The AI Confessional as a gathering series for leaders navigating the gap between AI's public narrative and their lived organizational reality, and developing the AI Accompaniment Cohort for leaders who want peer accompaniment through this threshold.
