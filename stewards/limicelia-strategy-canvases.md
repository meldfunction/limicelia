## LEAN STRATEGY CANVAS

**Goal**: Generate $350-450K earned revenue supporting Carol + BK as primary earners ($140-180K each) while building proof that accompaniment model works with mission-driven organizations.

**Obstacle**: Organizations seeking threshold-crossing lack frameworks to distinguish accompaniment from conventional consulting. Additionally, our revenue requirements position us as premium service, limiting accessible client pool.

**Strategy - Where We Play**: 
- Funder networks coordinating collective initiatives ($150-500K+ initiative budgets)
- Well-resourced worker cooperatives navigating governance transitions ($500K+ operating budgets)
- Mission-driven nonprofits at major inflection points ($1M+ budgets preferred)
- Geographic: US-based, virtual-first (Bay Area and DC region access)

**Strategy - How We Win**: 
Sustained accompaniment (3-6 months) building internal capacity + network coordination model bringing in specialized practitioners where Limicelia coordinates and takes 25-30% fee. Carol + BK bring complementary expertise: Carol's management science/contemplative containers + BK's systems thinking/post-activism frameworks. Both shaped by Present of Work experiments in gift economy and collective governance.

**Objective**: Accompany 12-15 mission-driven organizations through major thresholds while generating sufficient revenue to sustain Carol + BK at livable wages.

**Key Results**:
- 4-6 sustained accompaniment engagements completed ($50-75K each) = $200-450K
- 8-12 facilitated gatherings delivered ($10-18K each) = $80-216K
- 1-2 grant-funded programs ($40-80K each) = $40-160K
- Total realistic range: $320-500K
- Net to stewards after expenses: $256-400K

### USER PERSONAS

**Persona 1 - Funder Network Coordinators**

**Profile**:
- Lead collaborative funding initiatives (3-12 funders)
- Coordinate $500K-5M+ in collective grantmaking
- Struggle with distributed decision-making without centralizing
- Report to multiple boards/stakeholder groups
- Often understaffed for coordination complexity

**Jobs To Be Done**:
Increase network coordination capacity so collaborative initiatives deliver impact without individual funders losing autonomy → Secure continued funding, demonstrate value to boards, strengthen funder relationships, reduce personal burnout

**Why They'd Pay $50-75K**:
- Initiative budgets are large ($500K-5M+)
- 1-2% of initiative budget for coordination capacity is defensible
- Failure costs are high (network collapse, funder withdrawal)
- Cheaper consulting hasn't worked (creates more meetings, no capacity)

**Customer Discovery Questions**:
1. What coordination challenges are you experiencing right now?
2. How are you currently addressing these challenges?
3. What would "success" look like for network coordination?
4. What's the cost to your initiative if coordination continues to struggle?
5. How do you currently budget for coordination support?
6. What's your timeline for addressing coordination challenges?
7. Who else needs to be involved in decision to bring in support?
8. Have you worked with consultants/facilitators before? What worked/didn't work?

**Where to Find Them**:
- Peace & Security Funders Group
- Climate Emergency Collaboration Group
- Justice Funders
- Chorus Foundation Network

---

**Persona 2 - Well-Resourced Worker Cooperative Leaders**

**Profile**:
- Established worker co-ops (25-150 members)
- $500K-5M+ annual operating budgets
- Democratic governance experiencing strain at scale
- Member conflict or decision-making breakdowns
- Values-committed but structure-struggling

**Jobs To Be Done**:
Navigate governance transitions so cooperative stays democratic while making effective decisions at scale → Maintain member trust, fulfill democratic commitments, sustain operations

**Why They'd Pay $50-75K**:
- Operating budgets can absorb cost
- Governance breakdown threatens co-op survival
- Member education/conflict costs are already high
- Long-term investment in democratic capacity

**Customer Discovery Questions**:
1. What governance challenges is your co-op experiencing?
2. How many members are actively participating in governance?
3. What have you tried so far to address governance challenges?
4. What's at stake if governance issues continue?
5. How does your co-op currently budget for organizational development?
6. What would successful governance transition look like?
7. Who needs to be involved in deciding to bring in outside support?
8. What's your relationship with SELC or other co-op support networks?

**Where to Find Them**:
- US Federation of Worker Cooperatives
- SELC network
- Regional co-op associations

---

**Persona 3 - Mission-Driven Nonprofit Executive Directors**

**Profile**:
- Organizations with $1M-10M budgets
- Managing restructuring, culture shifts, or major conflict
- Values-aligned leadership but structure-misaligned operations
- Board pressure to "fix" organizational challenges
- Personal burnout from managing dysfunction

**Jobs To Be Done**:
Build internal capacity for distributed decision-making so organization navigates future thresholds independently → Reduce ED burden, empower staff, create resilient culture, meet board expectations

**Why They'd Pay $50-75K**:
- Budget size makes investment defensible
- ED turnover costs are higher
- Board expectations for organizational health
- Foundations increasingly funding organizational capacity

**Customer Discovery Questions**:
1. What organizational challenges prompted you to reach out?
2. How long have these challenges been present?
3. What's your board's perspective on these challenges?
4. What have you tried already?
5. What would need to be true for this to feel resolved?
6. What's your budget for organizational capacity work?
7. What foundation relationships do you have? Could any fund this work?
8. Who else needs to be involved in this decision?

**Where to Find Them**:
- Mission-driven nonprofit networks
- Foundation capacity-building programs
- Through foundation relationships (program officers refer struggling grantees)

## SOLUTIONS (Service Offerings)

### 1. Sustained Accompaniment (3-6 months) - $50-75K

**Structure**: 
- Bi-weekly or monthly sessions over 3-6 months
- Mix of full-team facilitation, leadership coaching, stakeholder engagement
- Capacity transfer not solution delivery
- Post-engagement integration support (3-6 month check-ins)

**What's Included**:
- Initial threshold assessment and engagement design
- 8-12 facilitation sessions (2-3 hours each)
- Asynchronous support between sessions
- Practice implementation frameworks and templates
- Final integration phase with handoff materials

---

### 2. Facilitated Gatherings (1-3 days) - $10-18K

**Types**:
- Strategic retreats (2-3 days)
- Stakeholder dialogues (1-2 days)
- Conflict navigation sessions (1-2 days)
- Network coordination convenings (1-2 days)

**Pricing Factors**:
- Duration (1 day = $10-12K, 2 days = $14-16K, 3 days = $16-18K)
- Preparation complexity
- Travel requirements
- Group size and complexity

---

### 3. Systems Convening (with Partner Practitioners) - $60-100K

**Model**: 
- Multi-practitioner team coordinated by Limicelia
- Carol + specialized practitioners deliver expertise
- Limicelia handles project management, client relations, contracting
- Limicelia takes 25-30% coordination fee, practitioners receive 70-75%

**Example**: 
$75K total project:
- Limicelia coordination: $18,750-22,500
- Specialized practitioners: $52,500-56,250

**Benefit to Limicelia**: Serve larger/more complex work without Carol/BK capacity constraint

---

### 4. Grant-Funded Programs - $40-80K per program

**Model**:
- Foundation pays Limicelia to work with cohort of organizations (3-8 orgs)
- Organizations receive capacity-building at low/no cost
- Limicelia designs program, facilitates convenings, provides individual support

**Example Structure**:
- 3-4 cohort convenings (full days)
- Individual org support between convenings
- Practice community coordination
- 6-9 month program duration
