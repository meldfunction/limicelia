# LIMICELIA & PRESENT OF WORK: STRATEGIC ESSAYS

---

## LIMICELIA STRATEGY ESSAY

Limicelia positions itself as **accompaniment practice generating livable income for Carol + BK** ($140-180K each annually in high-cost single-income household contexts) **while refusing extractive consulting models**. The strategic differentiation isn't methodology—it's how we organize and who we become through that organizing. Practitioners working with organizations bring embodied experience from Present of Work experiments (gift economy, peer learning, collective governance) as lived reality, not workshop concepts. **This reciprocal loop—consulting revenue supports practitioners who are shaped by commons participation—creates the business model**: Limicelia sustains the people, Present of Work shapes how they show up.

The 12-18 month goal balances **financial survival with values integrity**: generating $350-450K proves the model works at scale sufficient for two practitioners covering mortgage, rent, childcare, and basic living expenses in high-cost regions. **The biggest strategic risk is pricing validation**—will organizations pay $50-75K for 3-6 month sustained accompaniment when conventional consultants charge $15-30K for strategic planning? Year 1 experiments test this through direct conversations with 15-20 network organizations, pilot pricing with early adopters, and facilitated gatherings ($10-18K each) generating immediate revenue while surfacing organizations at genuine thresholds who might need deeper work. **Success means crossing viability threshold**: sustaining ourselves through values-aligned work at sustainable pace, not hitting revenue targets that burn us out or compromise integrity.

The strategic discipline question—Carol's experimentation across multiple territories and capacity to sustain initiatives—requires **ruthless prioritization**: experiments must serve strategy (testing accompaniment pricing, validating capacity-building outcomes, proving gift economy functions) not just explore interesting territories. Individual coaching tests spiritual/livelihood integration for Present of Work. Systems convening with partner practitioners tests multi-practitioner coordination. Network weaving tests commons onboarding. **These generate data about what actually works rather than consuming capacity on predetermined programs.** If Year 1 reveals organizations don't value sustained work or facilitated gatherings provide more sustainable revenue, **we pivot rather than push**, treating strategy as emergent navigation not predetermined plan. The times are urgent; let us move with discernment, building for 5-10 year timeframe while meeting immediate survival needs.

## LIMICELIA PRODUCT ESSAY

The core business problem is **proving value proposition before investing in infrastructure**: no clients, no validated pricing, no proof organizations distinguish accompaniment from consulting or that capacity-building justifies premium rates. **The riskiest assumption is economic, not conceptual**—organizations might intellectually appreciate sustained work while balking at $50-75K when they can hire consultants for half that cost. Additionally, starting a consulting practice while needing $275-375K combined income immediately creates pressure to compromise values for revenue. **Year 1 experiments must generate cash flow within 60-90 days while testing whether accompaniment model is viable long-term.**

The hypothesis-testing sequence prioritizes **survival first, validation second**: facilitated gatherings ($10-18K each, 2-3 day retreats) generate immediate revenue (delivering 3-4 in Q1 = $30-72K) while building relationships that surface organizations at thresholds who might need sustained work. These aren't "marketing"—they're legitimate service offerings and income generation. Simultaneously, outreach to 15-20 network organizations tests accompaniment pricing through conversations, not sales. If multiple organizations express interest at $50-75K, the model might work. If they balk but value facilitated gatherings, we focus there (10-12 gatherings annually = $100-216K sustaining both stewards). If neither resonates, we pivot to grant-funded programs where foundations pay us to work with cohorts of organizations, or we acknowledge Limicelia isn't viable and pursue other paths.

The solution set reflects **two-practitioner reality**: Carol + BK each have ~1,500 hours annually for Limicelia work after 20% to Present of Work participation. **This means maximum 4-6 sustained accompaniment engagements simultaneously** (each requiring 400-500 hours across 3-6 months) plus 8-12 facilitated gatherings. Scaling requires either (a) bringing in other practitioners on systems convening model where Limicelia coordinates and takes 25-30% coordination fee, or (b) saying no to work when at capacity. **The business model assumes scarcity, not abundance**—we can't serve everyone, so we serve those most aligned with threshold work and willing to invest in sustained capacity-building. Success = covering living expenses for both stewards while maintaining values integrity, not maximizing growth or building consulting firm. This is livelihood, not empire.

## PRESENT OF WORK ESSAY

Present of Work positions as **open-source knowledge commons that Carol + BK participate in, not organize**. The 80/20 capacity split (Limicelia 80%, Present of Work 20%) reflects Carol + BK dedicating 250 hours each annually to seed initial offerings—facilitating early practice spaces, creating content (podcast, blog), network weaving—while building toward **stewardship circle of 3-5 practitioners who collectively make decisions**. The strategic goal is Carol + BK becoming participants rather than permanent organizers. If Present of Work requires them to sustain it beyond Year 1-2, **it's not commons but Carol + BK's project**, which defeats the purpose. True success = practitioners stewarding commons collectively while Carol + BK remain involved but not indispensable.

The critical distinction is **Present of Work isn't revenue-generating entity**—it's slow-cooking model strengthening practitioners' ability to show up in transformation field, not enriching organizers. Limicelia is the only entity receiving revenue (consulting fees, grants, donations). Present of Work uses minimal infrastructure (donated Zoom, free-tier digital tools, occasional in-person gatherings at homes) and experiments with gift economy (monthly practice spaces where people give what makes sense), sliding scale (if intensive cohorts form), and open knowledge sharing (podcast, blog, toolkit freely available). **This means Present of Work success doesn't depend on financial metrics**—it depends on whether commons forms where practitioners co-create livelihood support infrastructure together.

The biggest strategic risk isn't **whether practitioners want community**—many burned-out facilitators seek alternatives to isolated consulting—but **whether commons actually forms or Carol + BK keep organizing everything**. Year 1 experiments test this by inviting 3-5 practitioners to join stewardship circle, tracking whether they propose offerings, hold practice spaces, or whether Carol + BK remain sole organizers with others consuming. **If stewardship circle hasn't formed by Q4 2026**, we assess whether Present of Work should continue, pause, or take different form. No judgment—commons formation is hard and takes time. The relationship to Limicelia creates **strengthening loop without dependency**: Carol + BK's participation in gift economy and peer learning shapes how they show up in organizational work (embodied experience, not techniques), while Limicelia revenue allows them to participate in Present of Work without requiring it to be self-sustaining. **Practitioners shaped by structure bring that structure to other contexts**—this is how becoming spreads, not through ideology but through people who've lived it.

## INTEGRATION ESSAY: How It All Fits Together

**Limicelia generates livable income** through organizational accompaniment work (sustained engagements + facilitated gatherings + systems convening) so Carol + BK can cover mortgage, rent, childcare, basic living expenses in high-cost regions. **Present of Work shapes how practitioners show up** in that consulting work through experiencing gift economy, peer learning, distributed authority as lived reality. **The two entities create reciprocal loop**: consulting revenue supports practitioners who participate in commons experiments, commons participation shapes practitioners who bring embodied ways of being to consulting work.

This isn't "side project" model where Present of Work is Carol + BK's nonprofit while they do consulting to survive. It's not "hobby" where they experiment with commons in spare time. It's **integrated livelihood strategy where work and practice aren't separate**. Carol + BK need $275-375K combined to survive in their geographic contexts. They generate that through Limicelia. They dedicate 20% capacity (250 hours each) to Present of Work not because they're altruistic but because **commons participation is what allows them to do accompaniment work differently**. Without practicing gift economy, they can't bring it to organizations. Without experiencing distributed authority, they can't facilitate it authentically. Without peer learning, they can't transfer capacity effectively.

The constitution and private agreement formalize **how Carol + BK collaborate while maintaining individual autonomy**. They don't need alignment on everything—Carol drawn to systems convening and spiritual/livelihood integration, BK to conflict transformation and practice pedagogy. **They share framings (form is politics, accompaniment not consulting, liberation through practice) while expressing different gifts.** The public constitution shows organizations how Limicelia operates. The private agreement handles financial arrangements, conflict resolution, and exit protocols. Both documents acknowledge **partnership has seasons**—ending well is success if collaboration runs its course.

The time estimates ground everything in **capacity reality**: 1,840-1,880 hours annually each, 80% to Limicelia (1,500 hours) and 20% to Present of Work (250 hours). This means maximum 4-6 sustained accompaniments simultaneously, 8-12 facilitated gatherings annually, plus business development, admin, and content creation. **No more can fit**—scaling requires saying no, bringing in other practitioners, or adjusting model. The quarterly breakdowns show **progression from viability testing (Q1) to sustained delivery (Q3) to integration and planning (Q4)**, with Carol focusing on systems convening and contemplative container design while BK develops conflict navigation and independent facilitation capacity.

**Success = Meeting not matching 2026**: approaching year honestly, testing riskiest assumptions (premium pricing, gift economy viability, stewardship circle formation), pivoting when experiments reveal different path, sustaining Carol + BK at livable wages while maintaining values integrity. This isn't building consulting empire. It's **creating conditions where work can be participation in life**—for Carol + BK, for organizations they accompany, for practitioners participating in commons. The strategy is patience, discernment, and honest experimentation. The times are urgent; let us slow down and build for long game.

## CUSTOMER DISCOVERY AS LIVING PRACTICE

The customer discovery questions embedded in our strategy canvases reflect **human-centered design approach to service development**. Rather than presuming we know what organizations need, we engage in rapid learning cycles through conversations with potential clients. These aren't sales calls—they're research conversations helping us understand:

- What problems organizations actually experience (vs. what we assume they experience)
- How they currently address those problems (what's worked, what hasn't)
- What "success" looks like from their perspective
- What they're willing to invest (time, money, attention)
- Who needs to be involved in decisions
- What timeline they're operating on

This approach honors that **our service offerings should emerge from understanding organizational realities**, not from predetermined consulting packages. Each conversation generates learning that refines our hypothesis about who we serve, what we offer, and how we price. If 15 organizations say "We'd value sustained work but $50K is impossible" while 3 say "$50-75K is totally reasonable for this depth," we learn which organizations are our actual market.

The discovery questions also serve **relationship cultivation function**: organizations approached thoughtfully with genuine curiosity (not sales pressure) may become clients later even if not immediately ready. They refer others in their networks. They remember being listened to rather than pitched. This slow-building approach aligns with our values around accompaniment over extraction—we're not trying to close deals, we're building relationships with organizations navigating thresholds.

## ON COMMUNICATION PREFERENCES

The constitution's inclusion of Carol and BK's communication patterns reflects **recognition that working well together requires understanding how each person operates**, not requiring sameness. Carol's preference for ensuring understanding connects to her cross-cultural communication research—she knows that misunderstanding creates more problems than over-explanation. Her acknowledgment that she can't control others' understanding or awareness reflects maturity about boundaries. BK's frustration with repetition and pattern of compromising boundaries reflects common challenge for practitioners trained in service—knowing when to stop a harmful process rather than continuing because someone asks.

**These aren't flaws to fix—they're information about needs and patterns.** Carol checks whether additional context helps or creates unnecessary length. BK practices naming boundaries earlier before resentment builds. Both recognize these as growth edges, not failures. This level of honesty in a public document serves multiple purposes: it models self-awareness and transparency about human patterns, it gives organizations working with us realistic picture of who we are, and it normalizes that practitioners are humans with patterns and preferences, not perfect facilitators with no needs.

The communication preferences section also addresses common collaboration breakdown patterns: one person feels unheard (Carol's concern about being misunderstood), the other feels like they're repeating themselves endlessly (BK's frustration with circles). By naming these upfront, Carol and BK create space to notice when patterns arise and address them with curiosity rather than making the other wrong. This is **threshold work applied to their own collaboration**—becoming aware of patterns, creating conditions for different responses, building capacity for navigation.

## ON PRIVACY IN AGE OF SURVEILLANCE

BK's privacy preferences aren't personal quirk—they reflect **principled stance about algorithmic surveillance and data extraction**. In age where social media platforms commodify attention and personal information, choosing not to participate is political act. The constitution honors this by not requiring equal public visibility from both stewards. Limicelia's visibility strategy adapts to both stewards' comfort levels—Carol may represent more publicly while BK maintains privacy, or they clarify which steward speaks in which contexts.

This has practical implications: marketing materials don't include BK's full biographical information, website may link to Carol's social media but not BK's, speaking engagements clarify which steward is available for which platforms. Organizations working with Limicelia understand they're hiring specific practitioners with specific boundaries, not interchangeable consultants. This models what we offer organizations: **respect for individual autonomy within collaborative structure, recognition that values can manifest differently, refusal to require uniformity for the sake of consistency**.

The privacy section also signals to practitioners that Present of Work won't require social media presence or public visibility. If someone values privacy for similar reasons as BK, they can participate in commons without exposure. This matters for building inclusive practitioner community—not everyone can or wants to maintain public profiles, and requiring it excludes people for whom privacy is survival strategy or values commitment.
