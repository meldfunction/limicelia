A / Page Anatomy — hover each section

Every service page is built
from the same bones.

Each card below represents one section of the public-facing service page. Hover to see what information type belongs there, why it exists, and how to know when you've got it right.

Section 01

Hero Headline

[ When the ________ is ________ the ________ back. ]

what goes here

What belongs here

One sentence naming the situation a client is in when they call. Not what you do — what they're experiencing. Written from inside their frame, not yours. The test: a potential client should read it and feel recognized.

Right form: "When [situation]." Not: "We help organizations [verb]."
Good signal: someone who has this problem sends it to a colleague.

Section 02

Lede — who it's for

[ Two sentences. Who. What shifts. ]

what goes here

What belongs here

Two sentences maximum. First: who specifically this is for (not "organizations" — what kind, in what moment). Second: what changes as a result of this work. Plain language. No jargon. No promises of outcomes you can't guarantee.

Avoid: "We use a holistic approach to drive systemic transformation."
Toward: "For executive teams who've changed their values without changing how decisions get made."

Section 03

Entry Scenarios — when you need this

[ 3–4 cards. Each = one real situation. ]

what goes here

What belongs here

Three to four cards, each describing one recognizable situation a client arrives from. Written as practitioner recognition — the client should see themselves in at least one. Ideally: real language from actual past clients, not invented scenarios.

Best source: things clients have actually said on first calls. Quotes, paraphrased situations, intake emails. Assign to limi-hq kanban.

Section 04

Engagement Types — what we do

[ 3–4 named engagements. Not tiers — types. ]

what goes here

What belongs here

The actual work — named by what it produces, not what we do. Three to four engagement types that represent the real forms this work takes. Not service tiers. Each has a description that conveys the depth and method, not the deliverable list.

Do not say: "Phase 1: Discovery (2 sessions)." Instead: "We surface the gap between formal structure and how authority actually flows — then redesign from the inside."

Section 05

Contrast — what makes us different

[ What most consultants do vs. what we do. ]

what goes here

What belongs here

A two-column honest comparison — not a marketing claim. Left: what the conventional approach looks like and why it fails. Right: what Limicelia does differently and why. Only include this section when the market distinction is genuinely important for a potential client to understand.

Not every domain needs this. Most urgent for domains where the market is crowded with bad versions (AI, org design, leadership).

Section 06

Related Work — what often runs alongside

[ 2–3 adjacent domains. Honest cross-references. ]

what goes here

What belongs here

Two to three adjacent domains that often run concurrently or arise from the same root. These are not upsells — they're honest recognitions that the work rarely lives in one domain. Only list domains that have a genuine relationship to this one.

For Org Design: Leadership & Culture almost always travel together. Conflict often surfaces in governance gaps. Don't list AI just because it's adjacent.

Section 07

Invitation — how to reach us

[ Not a pitch. A genuine next step. ]

what goes here

What belongs here

A short paragraph inviting contact — framed as genuine inquiry, not conversion. Domain-specific: the invitation should reflect the emotional state someone is in after reading this page. Email address only. No intake form. No "schedule a call" button if it reads as transactional.

Tone: "Tell us where your organization is. We'll tell you honestly if this is the work." Not: "Ready to get started? Book a discovery call."

B / Live Example — Org Design & Governance

What it looks like
filled in.

Below: the same seven sections, populated with current draft content for the Organizational Design & Governance domain. This is a working draft — not approved. Read through and note what rings true, what feels off, what's missing.

Live draft · Org Design & Governance · review and revise

01 · Hero Headline

"When the structure is holding the organization back."

02 · Lede

For organizations that have grown faster than their governance, or changed values without changing how decisions get made. We build structures that reflect how the organization actually wants to operate — not how the org chart says it should.

03 · Entry Scenarios

A board meeting where the same three people make every decision.
Staff who know the answer but have no place to put it.
An org chart that doesn't match how anything actually works.

04 · Engagement Types

Governance Structure Design — Mapping how decisions are currently made, where accountability is unclear, and what structures would fit how this organization wants to operate.

Role Clarity & Accountability — Surfacing the gap between formal structure and how work actually flows. Redesigning roles that reflect real authority.

Decision-Making Process Design — A differentiated system for which decisions need which process. Less energy deciding how to decide.

Governance Transition Support — Supporting the transition itself, not just the destination.

05 · Contrast

What most org consultants do: Arrive with a framework. Install it. Leave a report. The organization reverts within a year.

What we do: Build governance from the inside, with the people who will live in it. What remains is understanding, not documentation.

06 · Related Domains

Leadership & Culture — Structure changes rarely hold without parallel work on leadership behavior.

Conflict Resolution — Governance gaps frequently surface as interpersonal conflict.

07 · Invitation

Tell us where your organization is. We'll tell you honestly whether this is the work — and if not, what might be. No intake form. No deck. Just a conversation.

C / Co-Steward Prompts

Questions to answer
together.

Work through these in order. Your answers are the content. When all prompts are answered, you have a one-pager. Not every question has a right answer — some are designed to surface a real disagreement that needs to be resolved before the page is live.

Section 01 · Hero Headline

What does a client actually say when they call about governance? What's the first sentence of that call?

Current draft: "When the structure is holding the organization back." Does that land for the clients you've actually worked with? Or is it too abstract?

Section 01 · Hero Headline

Is the entry to this work usually from a leadership problem, a structural problem, a growth problem, or something else entirely?

The headline should reflect the actual entry, not the category of work. People don't call and say "we have a governance problem." They say something else first.

Section 02 · Lede

Who specifically is the first reader of this page? Name a real person or org type — not a persona.

The lede should be written for that person. If you can't name them, the lede will be too general.

Section 03 · Entry Scenarios

What are the two or three most common situations you've been called into for org design work? Describe them as the client would describe them, not as you would diagnose them.

These should be grounded in real situations, not invented. If you have an intake email or first-call note that captures the scenario well, paste it in. We'll shape the language from there.

Section 03 · Entry Scenarios

Is there a situation that often brings people to this domain that's surprising or counterintuitive — one that wouldn't obviously look like a "governance problem" from the outside?

Those are often the strongest entry scenarios. They signal to the right clients that you understand what's actually happening.

Section 04 · Engagement Types

Are the four engagement types listed (Governance Structure, Role Clarity, Decision-Making Process, Transition Support) the right four? What's missing? What's overstated?

This is about what you're currently able and willing to offer — not a comprehensive list of everything possible in this domain.

Section 04 · Engagement Types

For each engagement type: what does the organization have at the end that it didn't have before? Not deliverables — capacity, understanding, something structural.

If you can't answer this for any one of them, it may not belong on the page yet.

Section 05 · Contrast

Should the contrast section appear on every service one-pager, or only where the market distinction really matters?

Current hypothesis: yes for Org Design and AI (crowded markets), maybe not for Org Memory or Field Building (less crowded). What's your read?

Section 05 · Contrast

What's the most important thing that's different about how Limicelia does governance work compared to how it's typically done? One sentence, no hedging.

The contrast section only earns its place if the distinction is real and specific — not "we're more human-centered." What's actually different?

Section 06 · Related Domains

When you're doing governance work, which other domain almost always comes up? Which one surprises clients when it's connected?

The surprising connection is often more interesting than the obvious one. Both belong on the page.

Section 07 · Invitation

What's the right emotional register for someone who just read this page? What are they feeling, and what should the invitation say to that feeling?

Current draft is generic. The invitation should match the domain. Someone reading the Conflict page is in a different state than someone reading the Org Memory page.

Overall · Before Publishing

Is there anything on this page you wouldn't want a prospective client to read? If yes, what and why?

That's always the most honest editorial question. If it's there, it needs to be cut or fixed before the page goes live.
